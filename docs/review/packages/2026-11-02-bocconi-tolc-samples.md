#### percentage-changes: Successive percentage changes

- Section: Mathematics. Topic: Numbers: percentages
- Fingerprint: `b3dcc45923c1c2cb89cafdabf5d5ea2e7d02733aa0ef79e83185f1043cdfd204`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Question**

A course costs €240. Its price rises by 15%, and later the new price is cut by 15%. What is the final price?

- **A.** €240.00
- **B.** €234.60
- **C.** €204.00
- **D.** €276.00

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: B**

**Working**

The rise multiplies the price by 1.15: €240 + €36 = €276.

The cut is 15% of the new price: 15% of €276 is €41.40, so the final price is €276 − €41.40 = **€234.60**.

In one step: $1.15 \times 0.85 = 0.9775$, so the final price is 97.75% of the original, 2.25% lower.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** Assumes that equal percentage changes cancel out. They do not, because the 15% cut is taken from a larger amount than the 15% rise.
- **C.** Applies only the cut, and to the original price.
- **D.** Stops after the rise.

**Lesson:** Before using a percentage, name the amount it is a percentage of. Successive changes multiply: a rise of p% followed by a fall of p% always ends below the start, because $(1 + p)(1 - p) = 1 - p^2$.

</details>

#### absolute-value-inequality: An inequality with an absolute value

- Section: Mathematics. Topic: Algebra: equations and inequalities with absolute values
- Fingerprint: `e66b3cc7870ac9a36ebe8eabb3355a7031bea2a938f4f531741270eba335c85a`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Question**

How many integers $x$ satisfy $|2x - 3| < 5$?

- **A.** 3
- **B.** 4
- **C.** 5
- **D.** 6

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: B**

**Working**

$|2x - 3| < 5$ means $-5 < 2x - 3 < 5$.

Add 3 to every part: $-2 < 2x < 8$. Divide by 2: $-1 < x < 4$.

The integers strictly between −1 and 4 are 0, 1, 2 and 3: **four** of them.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** Solves only $2x - 3 < 5$, which gives $x < 4$, and counts 1, 2 and 3. That ignores the lower bound and loses 0.
- **C.** Includes −1, as if one end of the inequality were “less than or equal to”.
- **D.** Includes both −1 and 4, treating the strict inequality as non-strict at both ends.

**Lesson:** Rewrite $|A| < k$ as $-k < A < k$, keep each inequality sign exactly as given, and test a boundary value. Here $x = 4$ gives $|5| = 5$, which is not less than 5, so 4 is excluded.

</details>

#### conditional-probability: Conditional probability from a description

- Section: Mathematics. Topic: Probability: conditional probability
- Fingerprint: `db37c5e7de63f0ff3564e8051209487659d6ec04fa88424d0fc323285360a9c4`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Question**

Of 200 applicants, 120 took a preparation course. Of those 120, 90 passed a practice test. Of the 80 who did not take the course, 40 passed. One applicant who passed is chosen at random. What is the probability that this applicant took the course?

- **A.** $\dfrac{9}{13}$
- **B.** $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- **C.** $\dfrac{9}{20}$
- **D.** $\dfrac{13}{20}$

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: A**

**Working**

The applicant is chosen from those who passed, so they are the denominator: 90 + 40 = 130 passed.

Of those 130, 90 took the course. The probability is $\dfrac{90}{130} = \dfrac{9}{13}$, about 0.69.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **B.** This is 90 ÷ 120, the probability of passing given that the applicant took the course. The condition is the wrong way round.
- **C.** This is 90 ÷ 200, the probability that a random applicant both took the course and passed. It ignores the condition “who passed”.
- **D.** This is 130 ÷ 200, the probability that a random applicant passed.

**Lesson:** The group you are choosing from defines the denominator. Sketch a two-way table (course or no course; passed or not) and read only the row or column the question restricts you to. The same habit underlies Bayes’ theorem, which is on Bocconi’s topic list.

</details>

#### logarithm-equation: A logarithm equation and its domain

- Section: Mathematics. Topic: Logarithms and exponentials
- Fingerprint: `cdb5cb7d713379bf40b45a723ed6e1e32e24115578b1c2a13ac25f5d17b8e215`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Question**

Solve $\log_3 x + \log_3 (x - 6) = 3$.

- **A.** $x = -3$
- **B.** $x = 9$
- **C.** $x = -3$ or $x = 9$
- **D.** $x = 27$

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: B**

**Working**

Both logarithms need positive arguments: $x > 0$ and $x - 6 > 0$, so $x > 6$.

Combine the logarithms: $\log_3 [x(x - 6)] = 3$, so $x(x - 6) = 3^3 = 27$.

Then $x^2 - 6x - 27 = 0$, which factors as $(x - 9)(x + 3) = 0$, giving $x = 9$ or $x = -3$. Only **9** satisfies $x > 6$.

Check: $\log_3 9 + \log_3 3 = 2 + 1 = 3$.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** A root of the quadratic, but $\log_3(-3)$ is not defined.
- **C.** Both roots of the quadratic, without checking which ones the original equation allows.
- **D.** Solves $\log_3 x = 3$ and drops the second term.

**Lesson:** Write the domain down before transforming a logarithm equation, and test every root against it. Combining logarithms, squaring or multiplying out can create solutions the original equation does not have.

</details>

#### reading-supported-statement: What the passage supports

- Section: Reading comprehension. Topic: Explicit information
- Fingerprint: `908cedd5039f2369498d4d71f6df0ede1702aeb708dd2c57d9cb09d32bd5208b`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Passage**

> In 2025, average nominal wages in Country X rose by 4%, while consumer prices rose by 6%. Because prices rose faster than pay, economists estimate that the purchasing power of the average wage fell by about 2%. Some commentators concluded that households in Country X were worse off. Others warned that the figure describes an average: workers whose pay was renegotiated early in the year may have kept pace with prices, while workers on long fixed contracts are likely to have fallen further behind. Neither group disputed the published figures; they disagreed about what an average can show about individual households.

**Question**

Which statement is best supported by the passage?

- **A.** Every worker in Country X could buy less with their pay in 2025 than in 2024.
- **B.** On average, a wage in Country X bought less in 2025 than in 2024.
- **C.** Workers on long fixed contracts received the largest pay rises.
- **D.** The commentators disagreed about the published inflation figure.

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: B**

**Working**

The passage says that the purchasing power of the average wage fell by about 2%, which is what B says in other words. The figures agree: pay rose 4% while prices rose 6%, and $1.04 \div 1.06 \approx 0.98$.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** Turns an average into a claim about everyone. The passage says some workers may have kept pace with prices.
- **C.** The passage suggests the opposite: these workers are likely to have fallen further behind.
- **D.** The passage says neither group disputed the published figures.

**Lesson:** Match the strength of an answer to the strength of the text. Words such as “average”, “about”, “may have” and “likely” limit what you can conclude; an option that removes them, such as “every worker”, usually claims too much.

</details>

#### reading-main-point: A commentator’s point

- Section: Reading comprehension. Topic: Implicit meaning
- Fingerprint: `0580b9ad62b9735d0fea5dae26ee82cf90900c204b749af1d2002d099c5ae659`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Question**

Refer to the passage above. What is the main point of the commentators who “warned that the figure describes an average”?

- **A.** The published wage figures were measured incorrectly.
- **B.** Rising prices affected only workers on fixed contracts.
- **C.** One average can hide very different experiences among workers.
- **D.** Households in Country X were better off in 2025 than in 2024.

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: C**

**Working**

These commentators accept the figures but say they describe an average, then contrast two groups of workers whose experience differed. Their point is that the average does not show what happened to any particular household: answer C.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** They did not dispute the published figures.
- **B.** Overstates the passage, which says these workers are likely to have fallen further behind, not that nobody else was affected.
- **D.** Nobody in the passage claims this.

**Lesson:** To find a writer’s or speaker’s point, locate the sentence that explains why they disagree with someone else, then choose the option that restates it without adding a new claim.

</details>

#### rates-not-totals: Comparing rates, not totals

- Section: Numerical reasoning. Topic: Reading a table
- Fingerprint: `40e1b94236b1c5bfcb97ea0afb4ff31fa569d3ff7a7f19d40a721d8a097f9920`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Passage**

| Workshop | Places offered | Applications | Accepted |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 40 | 120 | 36 |
| B | 60 | 200 | 50 |

**Question**

A workshop’s acceptance rate is the number accepted divided by the number of applications. Which statement is correct?

- **A.** Workshop B has the higher acceptance rate, because it accepted more applicants.
- **B.** Workshop A’s acceptance rate is 5 percentage points higher than Workshop B’s.
- **C.** Both workshops have an acceptance rate of 30%.
- **D.** Workshop A’s acceptance rate is 20 percentage points higher than Workshop B’s.

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: B**

**Working**

Workshop A: 36 ÷ 120 = 0.30, or 30%. Workshop B: 50 ÷ 200 = 0.25, or 25%. The difference is **5 percentage points**.

The “Places offered” column is not needed: the question defines the rate using applications.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** Compares totals instead of rates. B accepted more people but a smaller share of its applicants.
- **C.** Uses Workshop A’s rate for both rows.
- **D.** Confuses a relative difference with a difference in percentage points: 30% is 20% higher than 25% (5 ÷ 25), but only 5 percentage points higher.

**Lesson:** Decide what the question asks for (a count, a rate, a difference in percentage points or a relative difference) and find its denominator before calculating. Columns you do not need are part of the test.

</details>

#### shares-of-different-totals: Percentages of different totals

- Section: Numerical reasoning. Topic: Reading a table
- Fingerprint: `8ff59be5f0577c4775b2ef7bc4ae28b2f39a85d3b21b037462df54318f9a3bc7`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Passage**

| Month | Visitors | Share who bought an exhibition ticket |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| March | 8,000 | 15% |
| April | 10,000 | 12% |
| May | 12,500 | 10% |

**Question**

A museum recorded its visitors for three months. In which month did it sell the most exhibition tickets?

- **A.** March
- **B.** March and April, equally
- **C.** May
- **D.** About the same number in all three months

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: C**

**Working**

Multiply each share by its own number of visitors:

- March: 15% of 8,000 = 1,200
- April: 12% of 10,000 = 1,200
- May: 10% of 12,500 = 1,250

May sold the most: **1,250** tickets.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** March has the highest share, but a share of the smallest number of visitors.
- **B.** March and April do tie, at 1,200 tickets each, but May sold more.
- **D.** Rounds too coarsely. The question asks which month sold the most, and 1,250 is more than 1,200.

**Lesson:** A percentage means nothing without its base. Turn shares into numbers before comparing them; here a falling percentage hides a rising number of tickets.

</details>

#### true-false-cannot-deduce: True, false or cannot be deduced

- Section: Critical thinking. Topic: A passage and a statement
- Fingerprint: `6d51e608ad5bb1d5e868cf8663d3cece98860bbaadd27323a7973ac0cbd33ef7`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Passage**

> The town council says its new street lighting has reduced burglaries. In the twelve months after the lights were installed, reported burglaries fell from 140 to 110. During the same twelve months, the town also put more police officers on night patrol.

**Question**

Statement: *The new street lighting caused the fall in burglaries.* Based only on the passage, the statement is:

- **A.** True
- **B.** False
- **C.** Cannot be deduced from the passage

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: C**

**Working**

The passage gives two changes that happened at the same time, the new lighting and more night patrols, and one result, fewer burglaries. Either change, both or neither could explain the fall, and the passage gives nothing that separates them. It neither confirms nor contradicts the statement, so the statement **cannot be deduced**.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** Repeats the council’s claim. The passage reports the claim, but nothing in it establishes that the lighting caused the fall.
- **B.** Tempting, because the passage offers another explanation. But another possible cause does not show that the lighting had no effect. “False” needs the passage to contradict the statement.

**Lesson:** Answer only from the passage. Choose “true” only when the text guarantees the statement and “false” only when it contradicts it; everything in between cannot be deduced. With a −0.33 penalty on these questions, this distinction is worth practising.

</details>

#### overlapping-groups: Statements about overlapping groups

- Section: Critical thinking. Topic: Data and statements
- Fingerprint: `7eeed01bff57873c7d2169e237c7f6e403714c03b1e7dc7bc29a7fcb4206e271`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Question**

At a summer school of 60 students, 38 attend a statistics workshop, 30 attend a writing workshop and 8 attend neither. Which statement must be true?

- **A.** Exactly 16 students attend both workshops.
- **B.** Exactly 8 students attend both workshops.
- **C.** More students attend only the writing workshop than only the statistics workshop.
- **D.** More than half of the students attend the writing workshop.

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: A**

**Working**

60 − 8 = 52 students attend at least one workshop.

Adding the two workshops, 38 + 30 = 68, counts everyone who attends both twice. So 68 − 52 = **16** attend both.

From there: only statistics 38 − 16 = 22, only writing 30 − 16 = 14.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **B.** Calculates 38 + 30 − 60 and forgets the 8 students who attend neither workshop.
- **C.** Only writing: 30 − 16 = 14. Only statistics: 38 − 16 = 22.
- **D.** 30 is exactly half of 60, not more than half.

**Lesson:** With overlapping groups, count each part once: total = only the first + only the second + both + neither. A quick Venn diagram on your scrap paper prevents double counting. Read limits such as “more than half” and “at least half” exactly.

</details>

#### necessary-assumption: An argument’s assumption

- Section: For the Law test: verbal reasoning. Topic: Identifying an assumption
- Fingerprint: `3c194b939064ec79fe1bcccee49c803b4739e88911208ab4007964f2266836ee`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Question**

A student association argues: “Moving our weekly meeting online will increase attendance, because members will no longer need to travel to campus.” Which assumption does the argument depend on?

- **A.** Every member prefers online meetings to meetings on campus.
- **B.** Travel currently stops at least some members, who would attend an online meeting, from attending.
- **C.** Online meetings are always shorter than meetings on campus.
- **D.** Other student associations have already moved their meetings online.

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: B**

**Working**

The argument says removing travel will raise attendance. That only works if travel is currently keeping away some members who would otherwise come.

Negate B: if travel stops nobody from attending, removing it cannot raise attendance, and the argument collapses. So B is necessary.

B does not guarantee the conclusion; other obstacles could remain. A necessary assumption is a link the argument needs, not proof that its conclusion is true.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** Stronger than the argument needs. It still works if only some members prefer meeting online.
- **C.** Meeting length is never mentioned and does not connect travel to attendance.
- **D.** What other associations did says nothing about whether travel keeps these members away.

**Lesson:** To test a possible assumption, negate it. If the argument falls apart, the assumption is necessary; if the argument survives, the option is too strong or irrelevant.

</details>

#### tolc-sample-log-maximum: The maximum of a sum of logarithms

- Section: TOLC-I sample questions. Topic: Mathematics: logarithms and quadratic functions
- Fingerprint: `95cb6204af6a9efd1d26ba81c4f1ab3f60343fd5511c37f5c0f83d1ba67e850d`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Question**

What is the maximum value of the function $f(x) = \log_2(x - 1) + \log_2(5 - x)$ on its domain?

- **A.** $\log_2 3$
- **B.** $2$
- **C.** $3$
- **D.** $4$
- **E.** $f$ has no maximum

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: B**

**Working**

Both logarithms are defined only when $x - 1 > 0$ and $5 - x > 0$, so the domain is $1 < x < 5$. There the two logarithms combine into one:

$$f(x) = \log_2\big[(x - 1)(5 - x)\big]$$

The product $(x - 1)(5 - x) = -x^2 + 6x - 5$ is a downward parabola with its vertex halfway between its roots 1 and 5, at $x = 3$, where it equals $2 \times 2 = 4$. Because $\log_2$ is increasing, $f$ is largest where the product is largest: $f(3) = \log_2 4 = 2$.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** This is $f(2)$, and also $f(4)$: trying whole numbers in the domain misses the highest point, at $x = 3$.
- **C.** 3 is the value of $x$ where the maximum is reached, not the maximum value of $f$.
- **D.** 4 is the maximum of the product $(x - 1)(5 - x)$. The function is its base-2 logarithm, so its maximum is $\log_2 4 = 2$.
- **E.** On its domain the product inside the logarithm is a downward parabola, which has a highest point, and $\log_2$ is increasing, so $f$ has one too. What $f$ lacks is a minimum: it falls without limit near $x = 1$ and $x = 5$.

**Lesson:** To maximise the logarithm of an expression, maximise the expression: the logarithm is increasing. Find the domain before combining logarithms.

</details>

#### tolc-sample-lockers: Four lockers in a row

- Section: TOLC-I sample questions. Topic: Logic: orderings and arrangements
- Fingerprint: `7fb74b531c0479d7738b611d53816e2f9d4ebe9a99a618c4f22db0938474f586`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Question**

Four lockers in a row are numbered 1 to 4 from left to right. Luca, Marta, Nico and Olga each have one of them.

- Marta’s locker is immediately to the right of Luca’s.
- Olga’s locker is not at either end of the row.
- Nico’s locker has a higher number than Olga’s.

Who has locker 2?

- **A.** Luca
- **B.** Nico
- **C.** Olga
- **D.** Marta
- **E.** It cannot be determined from the information given

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: D**

**Working**

Start from the most restrictive condition: Olga has locker 2 or 3. If she had 2, Nico would have 3 or 4 and the two lockers left for Luca and Marta (1 and 4, or 1 and 3) would not be next to each other, which the first condition needs. So Olga has 3, Nico, with a higher number, has 4, and Luca and Marta take 1 and 2, in that order. Locker 2 is Marta’s.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** Luca in 2 puts Marta in 3, and Olga, who cannot take an end locker, would need 2 or 3: both are taken.
- **B.** Nico’s number must be higher than Olga’s, so Olga would need locker 1, which is at an end.
- **C.** Then Nico has 3 or 4, and Luca and Marta need two adjacent lockers among the two left: 1 and 4, or 1 and 3. Neither pair is adjacent.
- **E.** The three conditions leave exactly one arrangement, so the answer is determined.

**Lesson:** In arrangement problems, start from the condition that leaves the fewest cases and follow each case to the end: a contradiction rules it out.

- Section: Esempi di domande del TOLC-I. Topic: Matematica: logaritmi e funzioni di secondo grado
- Fingerprint: `3103159c23c0fb77213aca60acf48498ac25e3670c811274860483838655550a`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Question**

Qual è il valore massimo della funzione $f(x) = \log_2(x - 1) + \log_2(5 - x)$ nel suo dominio?

- **A.** $\log_2 3$
- **B.** $2$
- **C.** $3$
- **D.** $4$
- **E.** $f$ non ha massimo

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: B**

**Working**

Entrambi i logaritmi sono definiti solo se $x - 1 > 0$ e $5 - x > 0$, quindi il dominio è $1 < x < 5$. Lì i due logaritmi si possono riunire in uno solo:

$$f(x) = \log_2\big[(x - 1)(5 - x)\big]$$

Il prodotto $(x - 1)(5 - x) = -x^2 + 6x - 5$ è una parabola rivolta verso il basso, con il vertice a metà tra le radici 1 e 5, cioè in $x = 3$, dove vale $2 \times 2 = 4$. Poiché $\log_2$ è crescente, $f$ è massima dove è massimo il prodotto: $f(3) = \log_2 4 = 2$.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** È $f(2)$, e anche $f(4)$: provando i numeri interi del dominio si manca il punto più alto, che è in $x = 3$.
- **C.** 3 è il valore di $x$ in cui si raggiunge il massimo, non il valore massimo di $f$.
- **D.** 4 è il massimo del prodotto $(x - 1)(5 - x)$. La funzione ne è il logaritmo in base 2, quindi il suo massimo è $\log_2 4 = 2$.
- **E.** Nel dominio il prodotto dentro il logaritmo è una parabola rivolta verso il basso, che ha un punto più alto, e $\log_2$ è crescente: quindi un massimo ce l’ha anche $f$. Ciò che manca a $f$ è un minimo: scende senza limite vicino a $x = 1$ e a $x = 5$.

**Lesson:** Per rendere massimo il logaritmo di un’espressione, rendi massima l’espressione: il logaritmo è crescente. Trova il dominio prima di riunire i logaritmi.

</details>

- **E.** Ne conta due per legame, l’–OH e l’–H, che però si staccano insieme, come un’unica molecola d’acqua.

**Lesson:** In una catena di $n$ unità legate in fila ci sono $n - 1$ legami: conta i legami, non le unità. Vale anche per i polisaccaridi e per gli acidi nucleici.

</details>

#### tolc-sample-oxygen-percentage: Una percentuale in massa

- Section: Esempi di domande del TOLC-F. Topic: Chimica: la mole e le formule chimiche
- Fingerprint: `b7d325965251ef78d77187ccfeed66a408ac1f0cbfc0ed1c532b5352e9f6968a`
- Status: not reviewed by a human subject expert

**Question**

Quale percentuale della massa del carbonato di calcio, $\mathrm{CaCO_3}$, è dovuta all’ossigeno? (Masse atomiche relative: Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16.)

- **A.** 12%
- **B.** 16%
- **C.** 40%
- **D.** 48%
- **E.** 60%

<details><summary>Key, working and wrong-option notes (solve it first)</summary>

**Key: D**

**Working**

La massa molare di $\mathrm{CaCO_3}$ è $40 + 12 + 3 \times 16 = 100$ g/mol. L’ossigeno contribuisce con $3 \times 16 = 48$ g ogni 100 g, cioè con il 48% della massa.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A.** È la quota del carbonio: 12 grammi ogni 100.
- **B.** Conta un solo atomo di ossigeno; la formula ne ha tre.
- **C.** È la quota del calcio: 40 grammi ogni 100.
- **E.** Tre atomi su cinque sono di ossigeno, ma la domanda riguarda la massa, e un atomo di ossigeno pesa meno di uno di calcio.

**Lesson:** Percentuale in massa = (numero di atomi × massa atomica) ÷ massa molare. Moltiplica per l’indice, e confronta le masse, non il numero di atomi.

</details>
