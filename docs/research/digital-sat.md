# SAT (Digital SAT) — verified specification

**Version covered:** Digital SAT, Bluebook two-stage adaptive (2026-27 testing year / 2026-27 admissions cycle)  
**Verified on:** 2026-09-18  
**Admissions cycle:** 2026-27 (facts verified against College Board's Fall 2026 / 2026-27 published materials; College Board's "Changes for 2026-27 Testing" page announces no changes to structure, timing, question counts, adaptivity, calculator policy, or scoring)  
**Region / delivery:** Global (United States and international test centers; identical structure for SAT Weekend and SAT School Day) — Digital, delivered in College Board's Bluebook application on a student or school device. Two-stage (multistage) adaptive at module level. Linear (non-adaptive) Bluebook or paper forms exist only as an accommodation and have different question counts and timing.

> This record is compiled from the official test maker's own published pages. Every factual row below carries a numbered source. Where the test maker does not publish something, this document says so rather than estimating it.


## Summary

The Digital SAT is a two-section, two-stage adaptive test delivered in Bluebook: a Reading and Writing section (2 modules x 27 questions x 32 minutes = 54 questions / 64 minutes) and a Math section (2 modules x 22 questions x 35 minutes = 44 questions / 70 minutes), for 98 questions in 134 minutes (2 hours 14 minutes) plus one 10-minute break between sections. Each module carries 2 embedded pretest (unscored) questions, so 50 of 54 Reading and Writing and 40 of 44 Math questions are operational. Adaptivity is at module level only: performance on the first (routing) module determines whether Bluebook delivers a higher- or lower-difficulty second module; within a module students may move freely, review, and mark questions for review, but once a module is submitted they cannot return to it. Scores are 400-1600 total and 200-800 per section; there is no per-question guessing penalty, but scoring is Item Response Theory based rather than a raw-count conversion, and the IRT item parameters, scale-score tables, and routing cut score are not published.

### Timing and breaks

Each of the four modules is separately timed with its own countdown clock: Reading and Writing 32 + 32 minutes (64 total), Math 35 + 35 minutes (70 total). Total testing time 2 hours 14 minutes. Exactly one scheduled break — 10 minutes — between the Reading and Writing section and the Math section. There is no break between Module 1 and Module 2 within a section. Unused time in a module does NOT roll over: when a module timer reaches zero the student is automatically advanced. A student who finishes a module early can either review within that module or submit and advance; time saved is not credited to the next module. The Bluebook timer can be hidden by the student, with a mandatory alert at 5 minutes remaining. Accommodated timing (+50%, +100%, >+100%) and extra/extended breaks exist but College Board does not publish per-module minute tables for accommodated forms in the sources verified here.


### Navigation and review

WITHIN a module: fully free. The student may move backward and forward between questions, answer out of order, skip, change answers, and review until that module's timer expires. Bluebook provides Mark for Review (a bookmark icon on any question), a Question Menu that lists which questions were skipped or marked for review and allows jumping directly to any question in the module, an Option Eliminator for crossing out multiple-choice answer options, Highlights & Notes annotation, and a Line Reader. BETWEEN modules: strictly one-way. "Once you move on from any module, you cannot return to it." This applies both to advancing early and to being auto-advanced at timer expiry. There is no cross-section review and no end-of-test review screen covering earlier modules. Note that the Question Menu is scoped to the current module (College Board's Bluebook tools page phrases it as "any question in the section", which in Bluebook's on-screen usage means the current timed part; the hard no-return-to-a-finished-module rule is stated explicitly in the Bluebook test directions and on the What to Expect page).


### Calculator

A calculator is permitted on the ENTIRE Math section — both Module 1 and Module 2, every question. There is no no-calculator portion on the Digital SAT (this was a deliberate change from the paper SAT, which had separately timed no-calculator and calculator portions). Two options, usable interchangeably: (1) the Desmos calculator embedded in Bluebook, which offers graphing and scientific modes that the student can toggle between at any point in the Math section, can be dragged anywhere on screen, and (as of 2026-27) can be resized; (2) the student's own approved handheld calculator. Only non-CAS calculators are allowed; College Board no longer publishes an allowed-model list and instead publishes a prohibited list. If a personal calculator fails mid-test the student may continue with the built-in Desmos. A formula reference sheet is available throughout the Math section. NO calculator is permitted on the Reading and Writing section.


### Adaptive behaviour

Multistage adaptive testing (MST) at MODULE level, not question level. Each section is split into two separately timed stages. Stage 1 (the "routing module") is the same broad easy/medium/hard mix for every test taker and contains half the section's questions, drawn from all four content domains of that section so all domains are sampled before routing. Based on performance across the whole routing module, Bluebook then selects one of two prebuilt second-stage modules: one averaging higher difficulty, one averaging lower difficulty. Both second-stage options still contain easy, medium-difficulty, and hard questions, in differing proportions. Routing happens once per section (so twice per test: once for Reading and Writing, once for Math) and the two sections route independently. Because routing is module level, the difficulty of question N+1 never depends on the answer to question N — which is precisely why free within-module navigation is possible, unlike in question-by-question CAT. College Board publishes qualitative facts about the algorithm (routing accuracy >93% and consistency >90% in simulation; break point "set around the median" section score; roughly a 50/50 split of test takers; deliberate under-performance to reach the easier module yields a LOWER score) but does NOT publish the numeric routing threshold, the IRT parameters, the difficulty distribution of each second-stage panel, or the algorithm itself.


## Scoring

### Raw scoring

There is NO per-question wrong-answer or guessing penalty. College Board explicitly distinguishes the current model from the pre-2015-16 guessing penalty and advises students to answer every question; blank answers earn nothing and are never better than a guess. Omitted answers simply score as incorrect for the purpose of the ability estimate. CRITICAL: the Digital SAT does NOT convert a raw count of correct answers to a scale score. Scoring uses Item Response Theory ability estimation over the operational questions actually administered, so the difficulty and other characteristics of the specific questions a student answered correctly affect the score — "Two students who answer the same number of questions correctly in a test section may earn differing section scores." The 8 embedded pretest questions (2 per module) contribute nothing to the score and are indistinguishable from operational questions to the test taker. Reported scores: Reading and Writing section 200-800, Math section 200-800, each in 10-point intervals; total score 400-1600 in 10-point intervals and equal to the arithmetic sum of the two section scores. No subscores or cross-test scores are reported. College and Career Readiness Benchmarks are 480 (Reading and Writing) and 530 (Math).


### Reported scale

Total 400-1600 in 10-point intervals; Reading and Writing 200-800 and Math 200-800, each in 10-point intervals; total = sum of the two section scores. The scale is maintained via IRT ability estimation plus concordance and vertical-scaling studies. College Board uses the word "linking" rather than "equating" for the digital-to-paper relationship, stating that the process "falls short of meeting the psychometric criteria for equating" because the two measure similar but not identical content. The actual IRT item parameters, theta-to-scale-score transformations, and any per-form conversion tables are NOT published. Percentile tables and Skills Insight descriptors are published in score reports but were not verified in this session and must not be reproduced or approximated by the platform.


### Not publicly documented — we do not reproduce these

- The IRT model family and the item parameters (difficulty, discrimination, guessing) for any SAT question — never published.
- The theta-to-scale-score transformation and any raw-to-scaled conversion table for any specific Digital SAT form. College Board deliberately does not publish these because the score is not a raw-count conversion.
- The numeric routing cut score / theta threshold between the higher-difficulty and lower-difficulty second-stage modules. Only the qualitative statement that the break point is 'set around the median ... section scores' and that roughly half of test takers go each way is published.
- The exact difficulty composition (proportion of easy/medium/hard items) of the routing module and of each of the two second-stage modules.
- The maximum and minimum section score obtainable via the lower-difficulty second-stage path, and the exact region of score overlap between the two paths. College Board says 'many midlevel section scores are, by design, obtainable from either path' but gives no numbers.
- The positions within a module at which the 2 pretest questions are placed, and whether pretest placement is fixed or randomized.
- How responses to pretest questions are excluded from the ability estimate in practice (only the outcome — that they do not affect scores — is stated).
- Whether and how partially-completed modules or omitted responses are treated differently from incorrect responses in the IRT ability estimate.
- Percentile rank tables and conditional standard error of measurement by score point for 2026-27 forms.
- The scoring rubric and scale for the Digital SAT Essay (available only in select U.S. School Day administrations) — not verified in this session.

## What we could NOT verify

Each item below disables a dependent "exam-accurate" behaviour in the product. We would rather offer less than assert something false.

- The exact routing threshold between the higher- and lower-difficulty second-stage modules. Only 'set around the median section score' and 'roughly half each way' are published. => The engine MUST NOT claim exam-accurate routing. Either disable routing entirely and deliver a fixed second module, or route on a clearly labelled house heuristic (e.g. percent correct on the routing module) marked 'our approximation, not College Board's algorithm'.
- IRT item parameters and the theta-to-scale-score transformation. => The engine MUST NOT report an estimated 400-1600 or 200-800 score as if it were exam-accurate. Disable scaled-score reporting, or gate it behind an explicit, documented, clearly labelled approximation.
- Any raw-to-scaled conversion table for any Digital SAT form. None exists publicly because scoring is not raw-count based. => No 'your raw 42/54 = 680' feature.
- The difficulty mix (proportion easy/medium/hard) of the routing module and each second-stage module. => Cannot build blueprint-accurate adaptive panels. Any adaptive practice panel must be labelled as an approximation.
- The positions of the 2 pretest questions within each module, and whether they are fixed or randomised. => Cannot replicate 'indistinguishable unscored items' authentically. Recommend either omitting unscored items from simulations (and disclosing the difference) or inserting them at random positions with a clear disclosure.
- The exact score range attainable from the lower-difficulty second-stage path, and the overlap region between paths. => No 'your ceiling is X because you were routed down' messaging.
- Percentile ranks and conditional standard error of measurement by score point for 2026-27. => No percentile display, no score-confidence-interval display.
- Per-module minute allocations under accommodated timing (+50%, +100%, >+100%) and extended-break schedules. => Extended-time modes must be built as a generic multiplier and labelled as such, not as an exam-accurate accommodation replica.
- Whether the Bluebook Question Menu's scope is strictly the current module (College Board's tools page says 'any question in the section', which conflicts in wording with the hard module-lock rule). => Implement the review panel as module-scoped, which is the conservative reading consistent with the explicit no-return rule.
- The exact SPR answer-matching tolerance College Board applies (e.g. whether .6666 and .6667 are both accepted for 2/3 — the published example table implies yes, but the general tolerance rule is not stated). => Author each SPR item with an explicit accepted-answer set rather than relying on a global tolerance rule.
- Bluebook's precise autosave interval and reconnect-recovery mechanics. => Design our own autosave contract; do not claim it matches Bluebook.

## Implementation notes

- EXAM CONFIG SHAPE. digital-sat needs four module objects, not two section objects: rw-m1 (27q/32min), rw-m2 (27q/32min), math-m1 (22q/35min), math-m2 (22q/35min), with a single 10-minute break node between rw-m2 and math-m1. Model the break as a first-class timed node so pause/resume behaviour is explicit.
- TIMER SEMANTICS. Each module has its own server-authoritative deadline computed at module start. Unused time NEVER rolls forward — on expiry the server closes the module and opens the next one regardless of client state. Emit a 5-minute-remaining event per module to mirror Bluebook's alert. Allow the learner to hide the timer but force it visible at T-5:00.
- MODULE LOCK MUST BE SERVER-SIDE. Once a module is submitted or expires, the server must reject any answer write scoped to that module. Refreshing, reconnecting, or replaying a request must not reopen it. This is the single most important exam-accuracy rule for the SAT and is directly testable.
- WITHIN-MODULE NAVIGATION IS UNRESTRICTED. Free back/forward, answer change, skip, mark-for-review flag, and a module-scoped question menu showing answered / skipped / marked state. Also implement an option eliminator (strike-through on MC choices, undoable) since Bluebook has one and learners practise with it. Persist eliminator and mark-for-review state across refresh; they are part of attempt state, not UI ephemera.
- RESPONSE TYPES. Whitelist exactly single_select (4 options, exactly one key) and numeric_entry. Reject any other type at the config layer for this exam.
- NUMERIC ENTRY VALIDATION. Implement Bluebook's rules exactly: allowed characters are digits, one leading '-', one '/', one '.'; max length 5 without a leading minus and 6 with; reject percent, comma, dollar, spaces and any other symbol at input time with an inline error; render a live preview of the parsed value beneath the field (Bluebook does this and it measurably reduces entry error). Mixed numbers must be rejected with the guidance to enter an improper fraction or decimal.
- NUMERIC ENTRY GRADING. An SPR item can have MULTIPLE acceptable answers. The question schema needs acceptedAnswers as a set, not a single correctAnswer string, and each item must enumerate its accepted forms (e.g. 2/3, .6666, .6667, 0.6666, 0.6667 for two-thirds). Accept both truncation and rounding at the fourth digit. Do not implement a global float tolerance and hope — the exact tolerance rule is not published.
- CALCULATOR AVAILABILITY. Boolean per module, not per section-half: Reading and Writing modules false, both Math modules true. Provide a graphing+scientific calculator (Desmos is the real thing; if we embed the public Desmos API, check licensing before shipping) and a formula reference sheet available on every Math question. Make the calculator draggable and resizable to match 2026-27 Bluebook.
- ADAPTIVE ROUTING. Implement two prebuilt second-stage modules per section (higher-difficulty panel, lower-difficulty panel). Route once per section on completion of module 1. The threshold is OURS and must be stored on the attempt alongside the panel id, so a refresh or resume replays the same panel and results are reproducible. Label the routing as our approximation in the UI. Never adapt question-to-question — that would break the free within-module navigation the SAT guarantees.
- BLUEPRINT COUNTS. Build panels against OPERATIONAL counts: Reading and Writing 50 operational (Craft and Structure 13-15, Information and Ideas 12-14, Standard English Conventions 11-15, Expression of Ideas 8-12), Math 40 operational (Algebra 13-15, Advanced Math 13-15, Problem-Solving and Data Analysis 5-7, Geometry and Trigonometry 5-7), split evenly across the two modules with all four domains present in each module. Only after the operational blueprint is satisfied should unscored filler be considered.
- UNSCORED ITEMS. Since pretest positions are not published, the honest options are (a) omit them and run 50/40-question modules, disclosing the difference from the real 54/44, or (b) include 2 per module at random positions, flagged unscored in the attempt record and excluded from all accuracy reporting. Prefer (b) only once the bank is deep enough that filling 8 extra slots does not force duplication. Whichever we pick, state it on the simulation page.
- SIMULATION GATE. A full Digital SAT simulation requires at minimum 50 + 40 = 90 reviewed operational items per panel path, and with two second-stage panels per section that is realistically ~180 reviewed items for one non-duplicating adaptive form. Until that threshold is met, do not present a full simulation; offer single-module timed practice (32 or 35 minutes) explicitly labelled as practice, which is honest and still pedagogically useful.
- SCORING. scoringModel = IRT-based and NOT reproducible. Report per-domain and per-skill accuracy, time per question, and raw correct counts. Do NOT emit a 400-1600 or 200-800 estimate. If product later insists, it needs a written methodology page, a visible confidence range, the word 'estimate' adjacent to every instance, and an explicit statement that College Board does not publish conversion tables. Omitted answers score as incorrect; there is no penalty beyond that.
- CONTENT ORDERING. Within a module, order questions easiest-to-hardest within each skill grouping (Reading and Writing) and easiest-to-hardest across the module (Math). Store an editorial difficulty label and keep it distinct from any empirically calibrated difficulty, per the brief's schema requirement.
- STIMULUS MODEL. One passage (25-150 words, sometimes a pair) per Reading and Writing question, owned by that question. No passage groups. Informational graphics in Reading and Writing are limited to tables, bar graphs and line graphs; Math graphics are broader. Store underlying data for every chart and render an accessible table alternative.
- RESILIENCE. Mirror Bluebook's guarantee that a connectivity loss costs no testing time: autosave answers locally, reconcile on reconnect, use idempotent submission keys, and keep the deadline server-authoritative so a client clock cannot be gamed. Test: kill the network mid-module, answer three questions, restore, verify all three persisted and the deadline did not shift.
- ACCOMMODATIONS. Build extended time as a generic multiplier on each module's duration (+50%, +100%) and label it 'our extended-time mode', not an exam-accurate accommodation replica, since per-module accommodated timing tables are not published. The published linear accommodation form (Reading and Writing 2x33 / 2x39min, Math 2x27 / 2x43min, no pretest items) is a separate, non-adaptive config if we ever want it.
- PUBLIC CONTENT. Every SAT fact page must carry the administration year ('2026-27 testing year'), a College Board source link near the claim, and an explicit separation between College Board's documented rules and our approximations. The 480/530 benchmarks are readiness indicators — never present them, or any score, as a Bocconi or other admissions threshold.
