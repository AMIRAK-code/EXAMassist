# Law School Admission Test (LSAT) — verified specification

**Version covered:** LSAT, post-August-2024 format (no Analytical Reasoning), 2026-2027 testing cycle  
**Verified on:** 2026-09-18  
**Admissions cycle:** 2026-2027 LSAT testing cycle (August 2026 through June 2027 administrations), feeding fall 2027 JD admission  
**Region / delivery:** United States, Canada, and international (LSAC); administered for JD admission — Computer-delivered through LSAC's LawHub interface. Historically test takers could choose live remote proctoring OR a Prometric digital test center; LSAC announced that starting with the August 2026 LSAT it is "moving toward in-center testing for almost all U.S. and international test takers," with limited remote exceptions for certain medical accommodations and for candidates documenting they live more than 180 miles / three hours from a test center. LSAT Argumentative Writing remains separate and is delivered exclusively online under remote proctoring. NOTE: LSAC's own LSAT FAQ page still described a free choice of modality at the time of verification — this conflict is recorded in briefDiscrepancies.

> This record is compiled from the official test maker's own published pages. Every factual row below carries a numbered source. Where the test maker does not publish something, this document says so rather than estimating it.


## Summary

The current LSAT multiple-choice test is four 35-minute sections: two scored Logical Reasoning sections, one scored Reading Comprehension section, and one unscored variable (pretest) section that may be either question type and may appear at any position. Analytical Reasoning ("logic games") was sunset after the June 2024 administration and replaced by the second Logical Reasoning section, so any AR/logic-games content is obsolete. A mandatory 10-minute intermission falls after section 2. LSAT Argumentative Writing is a separate, unscored, remotely proctored 50-minute essay (15 minutes prewriting + 35 minutes writing) that must be completed and approved before a score is released. Scores are reported on a 120-180 scale with no deduction for incorrect answers, all items weighted equally, and raw-to-scale conversion equated per form and never published in advance.

### Timing and breaks

Four separately timed 35-minute multiple-choice sections (LSAC Candidate Agreement 2026-2027: "The Test consists of four (4) separately timed, thirty-five (35) minute sections."). A single mandatory 10-minute intermission is given after the second section for all standard test takers, and LSAC states that test takers with the standard intermission are required to take the full 10 minutes. LSAC's FAQ describes the overall multiple-choice sitting as "approximately 3 hours for standard test takers" including administrative time. There are no other scheduled breaks between sections. Unauthorized breaks are penalised implicitly: if a section's time expires during an unauthorized break, the test taker cannot return to that section. LSAT Argumentative Writing is a separate 50-minute session (15-minute prewriting analysis + 35-minute essay), taken online on a different day, available starting eight days before the multiple-choice administration.


### Navigation and review

Within a section: unrestricted movement among that section's questions for the full 35 minutes. The 2026-27 LawHub UI shows a question bar listing ALL questions in the section (previously only 10 at a time), with separators marking which questions belong to each Reading Comprehension passage. Test takers can flag questions for return (flagged items appear in the question bar), eliminate/rule out answer choices, highlight and underline text across the stimulus, question stem and answer choices, adjust text size and line spacing, see time remaining, and use a per-question-set "Reset Response" control to clear selections and eliminations. Between sections: no movement whatsoever. The 2026-2027 LSAC Candidate Agreement states "During the time allotted for each section of the Test, you may work only on that section," and that if time expires on a section during an unauthorized break "you will not be able to go back to work in that section." Sections auto-advance; there is no end-of-test review across sections. Scratch paper is provided for the multiple-choice portion (three scratch paper booklets from the proctor) but is not permitted for LSAT Argumentative Writing, which supplies a digital notetaking area instead. NOT VERIFIED: whether a 5-minute (or any) time warning is displayed, and whether an end-of-section review screen appears before the section locks.


### Calculator

No calculator is used on any part of the LSAT — no section requires numeric computation. LSAC's test-day guidance lists calculators among the electronic devices that must be removed from the testing area, and states that the single largest cause of cancelled sessions is the presence of a prohibited electronic device. Scratch paper is permitted for the multiple-choice portion only. For the engine: expose no calculator at all, on any section, and do not model a "calculator-allowed vs calculator-prohibited" split as exists on SAT/ACT/GRE/GMAT.


### Adaptive behaviour

The LSAT is NOT adaptive at any level. It is a fixed linear form: all four sections are pre-assembled, every test taker taking a given form sees the same items in the same order, and there is no module routing, no item-level branching, and no difficulty adjustment based on performance. LSAC states that all test questions are weighted exactly the same and that "the total number of questions you get right is what matters for your score, not which particular questions you get right or wrong." The only form-level statistical machinery is post-hoc equating, which adjusts the raw-to-scale conversion for a form's difficulty after administration; it does not change what the test taker sees. The unscored variable section exists to pretest new items for future forms, not to route the current test taker. Any adaptive-routing feature in the engine must be OFF for this exam.


## Scoring

### Raw scoring

Raw score = the number of multiple-choice questions answered correctly across the three scored sections. LSAC: "Your LSAT score is based on the number of questions you answered correctly — your 'raw score.'" There is NO penalty for wrong answers: "There is no deduction for incorrect answers." Omitted/blank answers are simply not counted as correct, so they are scored identically to wrong answers — guessing is never disadvantageous. All items are weighted equally regardless of type or difficulty; the unscored variable section contributes nothing. The raw score is then converted to the 120-180 scale via a form-specific conversion produced by equating. LSAC's own assessment staff illustrate that the cutoffs move by form: on a slightly harder form you "need to get 56 questions right to get a 160," on a slightly easier form 57. LSAC does not publish the conversion table for any live form in advance (or, for undisclosed forms, at all).


### Reported scale

Reported score scale is 120-180 in whole-point increments, 120 lowest and 180 highest. Each reported score is accompanied by a score band: a range around the reported score derived from the standard error of measurement (SEM), which LSAC describes as "a psychometric statistic" estimating the average deviation present in test scores due to the imperfect nature of tests. LSAC does not state a single fixed band width on its current score-bands page; it says only that a test taker's actual proficiency is unlikely to be more than a few points higher or lower. Score reports also include a percentile rank reflecting "the percentage of test takers whose scores were lower than yours during the previous three testing years." LSAC reports that LSAT forms typically have reliability coefficients over 0.9. LSAT Argumentative Writing carries no score at all but the completed, approved sample is shared with law schools and gates score release.


### Not publicly documented — we do not reproduce these

- The item response theory (IRT) item parameters (difficulty, discrimination, guessing) used in equating and form assembly.
- The specific equating design and anchor-item structure used for each form.
- The exact per-form standard error of measurement and the resulting score-band width. LSAC's current score-bands page states only that the band is derived from SEM and spans a few points in each direction; it does not state a fixed number.
- The number of scored items on a current post-August-2024 form, and therefore the maximum raw score.
- The number of questions in each Logical Reasoning section.
- Which of the four administered sections was the unscored variable section on any given form.
- The exact content-blueprint proportions (how many of each Logical Reasoning question type appear per section).
- Percentile conversion tables for the 2026-27 testing year (LSAC publishes three-year percentile tables on a lag).

- Whether the on-screen timer can be hidden/collapsed by the test taker.
- Whether unanswered questions are visually distinguished in the question bar in the 2026-27 UI (the 2019-era digital-LSAT description mentions moving to unanswered questions, but that document predates the current platform).

- Exact score-band width. Do not display '+/- 3 points' or a 7-point band as official; LSAC's current page does not state a number.
- The exact verbatim LSAC wording prohibiting calculators (the dos-and-donts page was fetched and lists calculators among devices that must be removed, but a verbatim sentence was not captured). - Accommodated timing multipliers (e.g. 50% or 100% extra time) and accommodated break structures — LSAC's accommodations pages describe the request process, not a published table of standard time multipliers.
- Whether the unscored variable section's position is randomized per test taker or fixed per form.

## Implementation notes

- Timing model: four independent 35-minute countdown timers, each hard-locking at expiry with no carry-over of unused time. Insert a forced 10-minute intermission timer after section 2 that cannot be skipped (LSAC requires the full 10 minutes for standard test takers). Total simulated seat time 150 minutes.
- Navigation lock: implement per-section sandboxing. Within a section, allow free jumping among all items for the full 35 minutes; at expiry, seal the section permanently. There is no global review screen and no back-navigation across section boundaries. Do not build an end-of-test 'review all' pass.
- Item-level UI the engine must support to be faithful: a question bar listing every question in the section (not a 10-item window), with RC passage-group separators; a flag/unflag toggle whose state renders in the question bar; answer-choice elimination (strikeout) independent of selection; text highlighting and underlining across stimulus, question stem and answer choices; adjustable text size and line spacing; a per-question-set 'Reset Response' that clears both selections and eliminations. Note LSAC explicitly could not ship single-click radio-button select/eliminate, so a faithful clone should not assume it.
- Adaptive routing: DISABLE entirely for this exam. No module routing, no difficulty branching, no item-selection algorithm. The exam config should carry adaptive=false and the engine should not even instantiate routing state.
- Calculator: expose no calculator on any section. Do not reuse a shared 'calculator-allowed section' flag from SAT/GMAT configs.
- Scoring: raw = count of correct answers across scored sections only; unanswered scored identically to wrong; all items weight 1.0. Because there is no guessing penalty, the app should actively coach 'never leave a blank' and should not implement any omit-vs-wrong differentiation.
- Scaled score: the 120-180 conversion MUST be flagged as an internal approximation. Store it as a per-practice-form lookup table owned by the platform, versioned, and render it with a persistent disclaimer that real LSAT conversions are equated per form and unpublished. Do not display a score band, SEM figure, or percentile unless sourced from a published LSAC percentile table, and label the cycle it came from.
- Section composition generator: build every practice form as 2 x LR + 1 x RC scored, and optionally 1 unscored variable section of either type at a randomized position if the product wants full-length realism. If a variable section is included, it must be excluded from scoring and indistinguishable to the learner during the test.
- Reading Comprehension form assembly: exactly four sets per section; each set 5-8 questions; 3-4 single-passage sets and 0-1 comparative reading set (two related shorter passages). The passage_group response type must bind a set of items to one stimulus and keep them contiguous in the question bar with a visible separator.
- Response types: only single_select (five options, one correct) and essay are needed. Do not build multi_select, numeric_entry, quantitative comparison, data sufficiency or two-part logic for LSAT. passage_group is a grouping construct over single_select items, not a distinct response type.
- LSAT Writing: model as a separate assessment with a two-phase timer (15 min prewriting with a notes area, then 35 min essay). Unscored — the product must not emit a numeric score or letter grade for it and must not imply LSAC scores it. Disable scratch-paper affordances for this module to match the real restriction. If the product gates a 'complete score report' state, mirror LSAC's rule that an approved writing sample is required.
- Content sourcing guardrail: gate every LSAT content source on a post-August-2024 publication or revision date. Two stale LSAC-hosted PDFs (candidate-info-sheet.pdf describing five sections and a 15-minute break; the 2018 Digital LSAT Screen Layouts PDF depicting Analytical Reasoning) would silently poison a naive 'trust lsac.org' crawler.
- Accommodations: the platform can offer extended-time multipliers as a learner preference, but must not label any multiplier as LSAC's official accommodation grant, since published multiplier tables were not verified. Note that LSAC does not annotate accommodated score reports, so accommodated practice should produce an identically formatted result.
- Copyright: LSAC's question types and skill lists are used here as a tagging taxonomy only. Do not ingest LSAC practice items, passages or answer keys; all bank content must be originally authored against this taxonomy.
