# The ACT (Enhanced ACT) — verified specification

**Version covered:** Enhanced ACT — 2026-2027 administration year (national online + national paper; state/district from spring 2026)  
**Verified on:** 2026-09-18  
**Admissions cycle:** 2026-2027 national test dates: September 19, 2026; October 17, 2026; December 12, 2026; February 27, 2027; April 10, 2027; June 12, 2027; July 10, 2027. Scores from these dates feed autumn 2027 entry cycles (and rolling/early cycles earlier).  
**Region / delivery:** United States (national Saturday and state/district school-day testing) and international test centers. Verified for the 2026-2027 national testing year; international pages confirm the new Composite from September 2025 and "additional enhancements" from February 2026. — Both paper-and-pencil and online (TestNav). ACT design framework: "The ACT test can be taken online or on paper... Test mode options are not changing with the enhancements to the ACT." Online is a choice, not a requirement. The enhanced format now applies to both modes on national test dates (online from April 2025, paper from September 2025) and to ACT State and District Testing (paper and online) from spring 2026. As of 2026-09-18 the rollout is complete across all three channels.

> This record is compiled from the official test maker's own published pages. Every factual row below carries a numbered source. Where the test maker does not publish something, this document says so rather than estimating it.


## Summary

The Enhanced ACT is a fixed-form (non-adaptive) multiple-choice test with three core sections — English, Mathematics, Reading — plus an optional Science section and an optional Writing essay. The core test is 131 administered questions in 125 minutes, of which 108 are scored and 23 are embedded unscored field-test items; adding Science gives 171 administered questions (142 scored, 29 field-test) in 165 minutes. Every section is scaled 1-36 and the Composite is now the rounded average of English, Math and Reading only, with Science feeding a separate STEM score and Writing (2-12) feeding an ELA score. Math answer choices were reduced from five to four, calculators are permitted only on Mathematics, and there is no penalty for wrong answers.

### Timing and breaks

Section time limits are fixed and per-section: English 35, Math 50, Reading 40, Science 40, Writing 40 minutes. ACT states Composite scores now require "125 minutes of testing for the enhanced ACT rather than the 175 minutes required for the legacy ACT." For national testing, examinees taking only the core test are dismissed after Reading; those continuing "will remain in the testing room while the other examinees are dismissed after the reading test. There will be a short break, after which they will return to their seats" for Science and/or Writing, and "Examinees taking both science and writing will have a short break before beginning the writing test." Both optional sections are 40 minutes and are given in the same room. For state/district testing, "English, math, and reading must be completed in one sitting, flexible ACT testing policies allow breaks before science and/or writing," and states/districts may administer Science and Writing on a separate day from the three core sections. The exact duration of the enhanced-format breaks is NOT published; ACT's online student tutorial still describes the legacy pattern (15-minute break after mathematics, 5-minute break after science), which conflicts with the enhanced-format description in the design framework.


### Navigation and review

Free navigation WITHIN the currently open section, but no cross-section movement. Online (TestNav): "Select Next or Prev at the top of the Test page to move to the next or previous question"; an Item Navigation ("Nav") panel "lists all the questions in the test, and shows whether they are answered or flagged for review"; a bottom navigation bar shows answered (bold, grey background) vs unanswered (grey text, white background) questions and allows direct jumps. Answers can be changed: "The previously chosen answer is still selected." Flagging is supported for multiple-choice items only — "You can flag a multiple-choice question for review" — and "You cannot flag an essay question for review." Flags are purely a navigation aid: "all questions are counted towards scoring whether they are flagged or not." Section integrity is enforced: "You cannot end the test in the middle of a section," and the test ends "once you have completed all sections or time has expired for all sections." Tools available online include a highlighter, zoom, and an Answer Eliminator (cross out answer options). A 5-minute warning is shown in each section, and the timer can be hidden/unhidden by the examinee.


### Calculator

Calculator permitted on Mathematics only: "Calculators may only be used on the mathematics test, including ACT WorkKeys Applied Math." Not permitted on English, Reading, Science or Writing. Never required — "all problems may be solved without a calculator" — and ACT does not provide a formula sheet ("the enhanced ACT will not provide a formula sheet for the math test"). Permitted types: "any 4-function, scientific, or graphing calculator, if it is not on the prohibited list and it is modified... if needed." Prohibited: anything with computer algebra system (CAS) functionality, specifically Texas Instruments "all model numbers that begin with TI-89 or TI-92" (TI-Nspire non-CAS is permitted), Hewlett-Packard "HP Prime, HP 48GII, all model numbers that begin with HP 40G, HP 49G, or HP 50G", Casio "fx-CP400 (ClassPad 400), ClassPad 300 or ClassPad 330, Algebra fx 2.0, all model numbers that begin with CFX-9970G"; also handheld/tablet/laptop computers, pen-input devices (Sharp EL 9600 permitted), calculators built into phones, and QWERTY-keyboard calculators (this QWERTY rule is "not applicable to calculators provided in a secure test delivery platform"). Modifications required for programmable calculators (remove CAS programs/documents), paper tape (remove), noise (mute), infrared port (cover), power cords (remove). Online: "The Desmos on-screen calculator will be available," and "Examinees may still use a preferred calculator for online testing in lieu of the on-screen option." Calculator sharing is prohibited. ACT confirms the policy itself did not change with the enhancements. Policy document version: updated June 1, 2026.


### Adaptive behaviour

NOT adaptive in any mode or section. ACT: "While the ACT can be taken online, it is not a computer-adaptive test." Computer-adaptive testing was explicitly ruled out during the enhancement design because it "would make paper and online modes not equivalent." There is no routing, no module selection, and no difficulty adaptation. Embedded field-test items sit in fixed slots within a given form (they move between forms, not within an administration). Consequence: the engine must set adaptive=none for enhanced-act and disable all routing UI and persistence paths.


## Scoring

### Raw scoring

Raw score = count of correct answers only: "The number of questions you answered correctly on each test section is a raw score." No penalty for wrong answers and no penalty for guessing; omitted answers simply score zero — they are neither penalised nor rewarded. Maximum raw scores under the enhanced format are the scored-item counts: English 40, Mathematics 41, Reading 27, Science 34. Embedded field-test items are excluded — the enhanced test "includes embedded field-test items, which are not counted in students' scores" — and ACT's own scoring keys mark them "Not Scored". Reporting-category raw subscores are computed by summing correct answers within each category code (English: POW + KLA + CSE = 40; Math: PHM + IES = 41; Science: IOD + SIN + EMI = 34).


### Reported scale

Each section raw score converts to a 1-36 scale score via a form-specific conversion table: "Each ACT test section generates a single scale score between 1 and 36." The ACT scale "will remain 1-36 with no changes to ACT benchmarks or state-specific achievement standards." Composite = average of the three core sections only: "Your Composite score is the average of your English, math, and reading scores, rounded to the nearest whole number." The explicit rounding rule from ACT's enhanced scoring key: add English + Mathematics + Reading scale scores, "Divide the sum by 3. If the resulting number ends in a fraction, round it to the nearest whole number. (Round down any fraction less than one-half, except for averages lower than one; round up any fraction that is one-half or more. Also round up averages that are less than one.)" Science is reported as a separate 1-36 section score and is excluded from the Composite. STEM score = average of Math and Science, awarded only if Science is taken: "students will receive a STEM score only if they take the optional science section." ELA score requires English, Reading and Writing: "a test taker must take the English, reading, and optional writing sections to receive a combined ELA score." Writing is scored 2-12, as the rounded average of four domain scores (Ideas and Analysis, Development and Support, Organization, Language Use and Conventions). Superscore Composite is also now English/Math/Reading only, mixing best section scores across legacy and enhanced attempts; Science superscores are still reported and still feed STEM but not the Superscore Composite.


### Not publicly documented — we do not reproduce these

- Raw-to-scale conversion tables are form-specific and ACT does not publish a general or current-form table. ACT publishes conversion tables only for released practice forms; those tables cannot be used to score an original question bank. Any scaled score our engine reports is an approximation, not an ACT score.
- The equating / pre-equating methodology and the item-level IRT parameters used to build each form's conversion table are not published.
- The blueprint constraints used to assemble an operational form (difficulty targets, DOK distribution, passage-type mix beyond the published percentage ranges, matrix-sampling tier assignments for actual forms) are proprietary. ACT publishes an illustrative three-tier matrix-sampling protocol for Math but not the operational sampling rules.
- The positions of embedded field-test items within an operational form are not published. In ACT's released enhanced practice form the unscored English items are clustered at the start, while the design framework says field-test items are 'placed strategically throughout the section tests' — the operational placement rule is not disclosed.
- Writing essay scoring: the domain rubrics are published but the rater model, adjudication rules and any automated-scoring engine behaviour are not documented in a way that permits replication.
- Percentile ranks / national norms for the enhanced Composite are not documented in the sources fetched here, so the engine must not display ACT percentiles.
- The exact durations of the enhanced-format breaks on national test dates ('a short break') are not published.
- There is no adaptive routing algorithm to document — ACT is explicitly non-adaptive — so any 'adaptive practice' we build is our own pedagogy, not an ACT behaviour.

## What we could NOT verify

Each item below disables a dependent "exam-accurate" behaviour in the product. We would rather offer less than assert something false.

- Exact duration of the 'short break' between Reading and the optional Science/Writing sections on national test dates, and whether any break exists between Math and Reading under the enhanced format. ACT's own online student tutorial still describes the legacy pattern (15-minute break after mathematics, 5-minute break after science), which contradicts the enhanced-format description in the February 2026 design framework. DISABLE exam-accurate break timing; present breaks as untimed or clearly labelled as an approximation.
- Whether the paper (offline) administration permits the same free within-section navigation and review as TestNav. The navigation rules verified here come from the online TestNav tutorial. Paper testing almost certainly allows free navigation within a section, but no official statement was located. Do not present navigation rules as mode-specific.
- The exact split of English items between short and long passages, and the number of passages per English form. ACT states the enhanced English test 'will now feature a mix of short and long essays' but does not publish the counts.
- Number of Reading passages per form. ACT confirms passage lengths (two ~750 standard words, one ~650) and the presence of a paired/synthesis element, but the official reading description page does not state a fixed passage count for the enhanced form.
- Number of Science passages per form and the enhanced-format split across Data Representation / Research Summaries / Conflicting Viewpoints. Percentages found on ACT's science description page (25-35% / 45-60% / 15-20%) are not re-stated in the February 2026 enhanced design framework and may be legacy figures.
- Placement of the 'Visual and Quantitative Information' reading skill area under a specific reporting category. The PDF table columns could not be read unambiguously; ACT's reading description page implies Craft and Structure / Integration of Knowledge and Ideas but does not assign it explicitly.
- Whether Science can be selected or deselected after registration, and the precise registration-time mechanics for choosing Science/Writing on national test dates.
- Percentile ranks and national norms for the enhanced Composite.
- Accommodation timing multipliers (50%/100% extended time) under the enhanced format — not verified in this session.
- Whether international (non-US) test dates in the 2026-2027 cycle are offered in both paper and online modes, and what the February 2026 'additional enhancements for international test takers' consist of.

## Implementation notes

- EXAM CONFIG SHAPE: model enhanced-act as ONE versioned config with four selectable section bundles (core; core+science; core+writing; core+science+writing), not four separate exams. Attempt records must pin the bundle chosen, because Composite, STEM and ELA eligibility all depend on it.
- DUAL ITEM COUNTS: every section needs administeredCount and scoredCount plus a per-item isScored flag (English 50/40, Math 45/41, Reading 36/27, Science 40/34). Timers, progress bars and 'question X of Y' use administeredCount; raw scores and category subscores use only scored items. This is the single fix for the 'ACT totals do not match section counts' problem in the brief.
- TIMING: strictly per-section server-authoritative deadlines of 35/50/40/40/40 minutes. There is no global test clock and no borrowing time between sections. Emit a 5-minute warning per section to match the real interface. Total core testing time is 125 minutes.
- NAVIGATION LOCKS: allow free forward/backward movement, answer changes, direct jumps and flagging WITHIN the active section; hard-lock any return to a completed section server-side. Block 'submit section early' if we want fidelity — ACT states you cannot end the test in the middle of a section. Flags must be persisted per attempt but must never influence scoring.
- CALCULATOR AVAILABILITY: calculatorPolicy must be per-section, not per-exam — enabled on Mathematics only, disabled on English, Reading, Science and Writing. If we ship an on-screen calculator, a Desmos-style graphing calculator on Math only is the faithful choice. Do NOT provide a formula sheet on ACT math.
- ADAPTIVE ROUTING: set adaptive=none and disable every routing code path for this exam. Persisting 'adaptive routing decisions' per attempt (required by the brief for other exams) is a no-op here — persist only question order. Any adaptive drill mode must be labelled as our own study feature, not an ACT behaviour, in UI copy and on public pages.
- SCORING PIPELINE: rawScore = count correct over scored items; no wrong-answer penalty; omitted == incorrect for scoring but must be tracked separately for analytics. Category subscores sum correct answers per reporting-category tag (English POW+KLA+CSE=40; Math PHM+IES=41; Science IOD+SIN+EMI=34). Composite = round(avg(englishScale, mathScale, readingScale)) with ACT's explicit half-up rule. Gate STEM on math+science present and ELA on english+reading+writing present.
- SCALED SCORES: we cannot compute real 1-36 scale scores for original items — conversion tables are form-specific and unpublished. Default the ACT results screen to raw score, percent correct, per-category accuracy and pacing. If a 1-36 estimate is shown at all, it must sit behind a documented methodology and an adjacent 'estimate, not an ACT score' label, and percentiles must be suppressed entirely.
- ITEM AUTHORING CONSTRAINTS: all ACT multiple-choice items must have exactly 4 options. Reject or quarantine any 5-option math item as stale legacy content. ACT convention alternates A/B/C/D and F/G/H/J between odd and even items — worth mirroring for realism but it is presentational only. English items now carry an explicit stem, so legacy-style 'NO CHANGE' items with no question text should be re-authored with a stem.
- PASSAGE GROUPS: English, Reading and Science all need shared-stimulus grouping. Reading needs paired-passage support (synthesis across two texts) and at least one passage carrying a visual or quantitative element. Science needs table/graph/diagram stimuli with an accessible data table stored alongside, per the brief's chart rule.
- FIELD-TEST SIMULATION: if we mirror embedded unscored items for realism, their positions must be fixed in the pinned form version and invisible to the learner during the attempt, but the results screen must disclose which items were unscored. Do not cluster them at the start of English just because ACT's released practice form does — that placement is form-specific and ACT does not publish the operational rule.
- BREAKS: implement breaks as configurable and default them OFF or untimed for ACT, with a visible note that enhanced-format break durations are not published and official sources conflict. Do not hard-code the legacy 15/5-minute pattern.
- RESPONSE TYPE ALLOWLIST: single_select, passage_group, essay only. The ACT config must not expose multi_select, numeric_entry, quantitative_comparison, data_sufficiency or two_part renderers.
- ESSAY HANDLING: the Writing prompt cannot be flagged, is 40 minutes, and produces four domain scores of 2-12 averaged and rounded. Automated essay scoring is not replicable from published material — offer rubric-guided self-assessment or clearly-labelled AI feedback, never a claimed ACT Writing score.
- PUBLIC CONTENT VERSIONING: every ACT fact page must state 'Enhanced ACT, 2026-2027 administration year' and note that material describing a 215-question, 175-minute, science-mandatory ACT is legacy. Composite-from-three-sections and four-option math are the two changes most likely to be wrong in competing prep content, so lead with them.
