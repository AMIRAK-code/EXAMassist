# GRE General Test — verified specification

**Version covered:** Shorter GRE General Test (format effective September 22, 2023; verified for the 2026–27 administration year)  
**Verified on:** 2026-09-18  
**Admissions cycle:** 2026–27 admissions cycle. The ETS score-interpretation publication fetched is explicitly titled "Interpreting Your GRE Scores: 2026-27", and the Information Bulletin fetched lists Subject Test dates through April–May 2027, confirming it is the current-cycle bulletin.  
**Region / delivery:** Global (ETS, worldwide). Verified against the US-region ets.org pages plus ETS India and ETS Europe PDF mirrors of the Information Bulletin and score-interpretation publications. — Computer-delivered (test center or at home). The Analytical Writing task is typed in a basic ETS word processor with insert, delete, cut-and-paste and undo, and no spellchecker or grammar checker. CAUTION: the ETS Verbal Reasoning page still contains legacy text saying Select-in-Passage questions "don't appear on the paper-delivered test". I could NOT verify whether a paper-delivered GRE General Test is still offered in 2026, so treat that sentence as stale and do not build a paper mode from it.

> This record is compiled from the official test maker's own published pages. Every factual row below carries a numbered source. Where the test maker does not publish something, this document says so rather than estimating it.


## Summary

The current GRE General Test runs about 1 hour and 58 minutes (118 minutes) across five sections: one 30-minute Analytical Writing "Analyze an Issue" task, two Verbal Reasoning sections (12 questions/18 minutes, then 15 questions/23 minutes) and two Quantitative Reasoning sections (12 questions/21 minutes, then 15 questions/26 minutes), for 54 scored multiple-choice/numeric items plus one essay. Analytical Writing is always first; the Verbal and Quantitative sections may appear in any order after it. Verbal and Quantitative are section-level adaptive: the first section of each measure is average difficulty and the second section's difficulty depends on performance on the first. Within a section the taker can move forward and backward, mark and review, and change answers; a basic on-screen calculator is available for Quantitative Reasoning only. Verbal and Quantitative are reported 130–170 in one-point increments, Analytical Writing 0–6 in half-point increments, with nothing subtracted for incorrect answers.

### Timing and breaks

Each section is separately timed: Analytical Writing 30 min; Verbal section 1 18 min; Verbal section 2 23 min; Quantitative section 1 21 min; Quantitative section 2 26 min. Total 118 minutes. NO SCHEDULED BREAK is documented for the current format. This is established by absence rather than by an explicit statement: on the ETS test-structure page the only occurrence of the word "break" is in the pre-September-22-2023 block ("There are six sections with a 10-minute break following the third section"), and neither the current-format block nor the Information Bulletin's "Test Structure Beginning September 22, 2023" section mentions any break. ETS does not publish a sentence explicitly saying "there is no break" in the shorter test, so treat the no-break configuration as strongly evidenced but not directly quoted.


### Navigation and review

Free navigation WITHIN a section. ETS: "The advanced adaptive design of the GRE General Test allows you to move forward and backward throughout an entire section." Specific published features: "preview and review capabilities within a section"; "'Mark' and 'Review' features to tag questions, so you can skip them and return later if you have time remaining in the section"; "the ability to change/edit answers within a section". The strategies page adds that the taker can "view a complete list of all the questions in the section on which you're working, so you can see the questions you've answered and those you've marked for review" and can "review questions you've already answered and change your answers, provided you still have time remaining to work on that section". NOT PUBLISHED: ETS nowhere states explicitly that a completed section is locked and cannot be revisited. Cross-section locking is implied by section-level adaptation (the second section cannot be selected until the first is complete) but is not quotable, so it is listed under unverifiable.


### Calculator

A basic on-screen calculator is provided for the Quantitative Reasoning measure only ("an on-screen calculator for the Quantitative Reasoning section"; "You're provided with a basic on-screen calculator on the Quantitative Reasoning measure"). ETS does not publish an explicit statement that no calculator is available in Verbal Reasoning or Analytical Writing; that is inferred from the calculator being scoped to Quantitative. Published calculator behaviour (from the official Guidelines to Using the On-screen Calculator PDF): respects order of operations — parentheses, exponentiation including square roots, multiplication and division left to right, then addition and subtraction left to right; has parentheses plus one memory location with memory recall, memory clear and memory sum buttons; "The calculator displays up to eight digits"; results greater than 99,999,999 display ERROR; division by zero or the square root of a negative number displays ERROR, cleared only with the clear button; a positive result less than 0.0000001 displays 0. Transfer Display: "The Transfer Display button can be used on Numeric Entry questions with a single answer box", and "The Transfer Display button on the calculator cannot be used for a fraction."


### Adaptive behaviour

Section-level adaptive (NOT question-level / item-level). ETS: "The Verbal Reasoning and Quantitative Reasoning sections are section-level adaptive. The first section of each measure (i.e., Verbal and Quantitative) is of average difficulty. The difficulty level of the second section of each of the measures depends on your overall performance on the first section." Worked example given by ETS: "if you do very well on the first Quantitative Reasoning section, the second section of Quantitative Reasoning will be at a higher level of difficulty." Scoring consequence, per ETS: "The scoring for the Verbal Reasoning and Quantitative Reasoning measures takes into consideration the total number of questions answered correctly across the two sections, as well as the difficulty level of the sections." The scoring page independently confirms: "The Verbal Reasoning and Quantitative Reasoning measures are section-level adaptive. This means the computer selects the second operational section of a measure based on your performance on the first section." Analytical Writing is not adaptive. NOT PUBLISHED: the number of difficulty tiers available for the second section, the routing thresholds, and the routing algorithm itself.


## Scoring

### Raw scoring

Raw score = count of correct answers. ETS: "For each of the two measures, a raw score is computed. The raw score is the number of questions you answered correctly." No wrong-answer penalty: "Nothing is subtracted from your score for incorrect answers", and ETS advises "To maximize your scores on the Verbal Reasoning and Quantitative Reasoning measures, it's best to answer every question." Omitted answers therefore simply score zero; they are not penalised. All items weigh the same within a section: "Within each section, all questions contribute equally to the final score." No partial credit on multi-answer items: for Verbal select-one-or-more, "to gain credit for these questions, you must select all the correct answers; there is no credit for partially correct answers"; Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence likewise state "There's no credit for partially correct answers." If a measure is left entirely unanswered: "If you have not answered at least one question within a measure, an NS (No Score) is reported for that measure."


### Reported scale

Verbal Reasoning and Quantitative Reasoning: 130–170 in one-point increments, each scaled separately. Analytical Writing: 0–6 in half-point increments, a single reported score for the one Analyze an Issue task, evaluated on a 6-point holistic scale with input from the e-rater scoring engine. NS (No Score) is reported for a measure with no answered questions, and NS is also an Analytical Writing outcome ("You produced no text whatsoever"); score 0 covers off-task, copied, foreign-language or indecipherable responses. Raw-to-scale conversion is by equating: "The raw score is converted to a scaled score through a process known as equating. The equating process accounts for minor variations in difficulty among the different test editions as well as the differences in difficulty introduced by the section-level adaptation." ETS states the intended invariance — a given scaled score reflects the same performance level regardless of which second section was selected — but publishes NO conversion table.


### Not publicly documented — we do not reproduce these

- Raw-to-scaled conversion tables for Verbal Reasoning and Quantitative Reasoning. ETS describes equating in prose but publishes no raw-score-to-130-170 lookup table for any form, so an exam-accurate scaled score cannot be computed.
- The equating model itself (linear, equipercentile, IRT true-score, or other) and its anchor/common-item design.
- IRT item parameters (discrimination, difficulty, guessing) for any GRE item, and the item pool statistics.
- The section-level adaptive routing algorithm: how the first-section performance statistic is computed, the cut thresholds, and how many distinct difficulty levels the second section can take.
- How the difficulty level of the delivered second section is quantitatively combined with the raw count of correct answers to produce the scaled score.
- e-rater feature set and weights, the human-rater/e-rater adjudication rule, and the discrepancy threshold that triggers a second human reading for Analytical Writing.
- Percentile-rank tables are published as summary statistics only for a fixed reference window (test takers from July 1, 2022 through June 30, 2025); the underlying score distribution is not published at raw-score granularity.
- Whether the two sections of a given measure must be delivered adjacently, or may be separated by a section of the other measure.

## What we could NOT verify

Each item below disables a dependent "exam-accurate" behaviour in the product. We would rather offer less than assert something false.

- Whether a completed section is permanently locked. ETS documents free navigation WITHIN a section but never states that the taker cannot return to a prior section. Cross-section locking is implied by section-level adaptation but is not quotable — do not present a 'sections are locked' rule to learners as an ETS fact.
- Whether the two sections of a measure are delivered adjacently. ETS says only that Verbal and Quantitative sections 'may appear in any order after the Analytical Writing section'. It is not published whether an order such as V1, Q1, V2, Q2 can occur, so a simulator must not claim a canonical AW-V-V-Q-Q order.
- The section-level adaptive routing algorithm: the statistic computed from section 1, the number of distinct difficulty levels available for section 2, and the cut thresholds. Disable any feature that claims to reproduce real GRE routing.
- IRT item parameters and the item pool calibration. No exam-accurate difficulty estimate can be attached to a bank item.
- Raw-to-scaled conversion tables for 130-170. No accurate scaled score can be produced; any 130-170 number the app shows is an estimate, not an ETS score.
- How raw count and delivered-section difficulty are combined into the scaled score.
- e-rater feature weights, the human/e-rater adjudication rule and the discrepancy threshold for Analytical Writing. Automated 0-6 essay scoring cannot be made exam-accurate.
- Whether any scheduled break exists in the current five-section format. Evidenced by absence (the only 'break' text on the ETS structure page belongs to the pre-September-2023 format), but never stated affirmatively by ETS.
- Whether a paper-delivered GRE General Test is still offered in 2026. The ETS Verbal Reasoning page still refers to a 'paper-delivered test' when explaining Select-in-Passage; this appears to be legacy text and was not confirmed elsewhere.
- The exact per-section mix of question types (how many Reading Comprehension vs Text Completion vs Sentence Equivalence items in a 12- or 15-question Verbal section; how many Quantitative Comparison vs Data Interpretation items in a Quantitative section). ETS publishes only that 'about half' of Verbal is passage-based.
- Whether the on-screen calculator is technically unavailable in Verbal and Analytical Writing. ETS scopes it to Quantitative but never states it is blocked elsewhere.
- Character or word limits for the Analytical Writing response, and whether the word processor displays a word count.

## Implementation notes

- TIMING MODEL: five independently timed sections, no shared pool, no scheduled break. Configure 30 / 18 / 23 / 21 / 26 minutes. Expiry of a section's timer must auto-advance to the next section; do not roll unused time forward, as ETS publishes per-section allotments, not a global budget.
- SECTION ORDER: pin Analytical Writing to position 1. Treat the V/Q ordering as configurable with the constraint section1(measure) precedes section2(measure). Ship adjacent pairs as default and flag the ordering as 'representative, not ETS-specified' in any exam-accuracy claim.
- ADAPTIVE ROUTING: the engine needs a two-tier-or-more difficulty pool per measure and a routing hook after section 1. Because ETS publishes neither the tier count nor the thresholds, the routing rule must live in a clearly-labelled heuristic config (e.g. percent-correct bands) and the UI must not claim it mirrors ETS. Section 1 must be drawn from an 'average difficulty' pool by definition.
- NAVIGATION LOCKS: enable back/forward, answer change and Mark/Review WITHIN a section; build the section review screen listing every item with answered/marked state. Lock a section on submit or timer expiry — required for routing — but treat that lock as a product rule, not a cited ETS rule.
- CALCULATOR AVAILABILITY: bind calculator availability to the section's measure, not globally. Quantitative sections only. Implement the documented behaviours or the sim will be wrong in ways learners notice: strict order of operations (parentheses, exponentiation/roots, mul/div left-to-right, add/sub left-to-right — note many consumer calculators differ), parentheses, one memory slot with MR/MC/M+, eight-digit display, ERROR above 99,999,999 and on division by zero or sqrt of a negative (clearable only with the clear key), and display of 0 for positive results below 1e-7.
- TRANSFER DISPLAY: implement the Transfer Display button, enabled only on single-box Numeric Entry items and explicitly disabled on two-box fraction items. This is a real, testable behavioural difference.
- NUMERIC ENTRY GRADING: grade by numeric value, not string. Accept equivalent forms (2.5 == 2.50). For fractions, accept any unreduced equivalent, reject a decimal point in either box, and accept a hyphen in either box as the negative sign. When the item carries no rounding instruction, require the exact value; when it does, grade against the rounded value at the stated precision. Input masks must permit hyphen and period only as specified.
- RESPONSE TYPES TO BUILD: quantitative_comparison with four fixed, non-shuffleable answer choices (option order is part of the item type and must never be randomized); single_select with five choices; multi_select with two distinct shapes — Verbal RC (three choices, one to three correct, count not disclosed) and Sentence Equivalence (six choices, exactly two, count disclosed); numeric_entry in both single-box and two-box variants; passage_group for RC passages (one to six items sharing a passage) and for Data Interpretation sets (items sharing a table/graph); essay.
- MISSING RESPONSE TYPES: the supplied enum has no Select-in-Passage type. GRE requires it — the learner clicks a word to select a whole sentence, and in multi-paragraph passages the selectable region is restricted to one or two specified paragraphs. This needs a new sentence-tokenized passage renderer with a per-item selectable-paragraph range. Also generalize two_part into an n-blank Text Completion type (1-3 blanks; 5 choices if one blank, 3 per blank if two or three; blanks independent; all-or-nothing scoring).
- SCORING ENGINE: score all multi-answer items all-or-nothing, with no partial credit. Weight every item within a section equally. Never subtract for a wrong answer, and treat an omission identically to a wrong answer — the coaching copy should actively tell learners to guess rather than leave blanks, which matches ETS's own advice. Emit NS rather than a numeric score when a measure has zero answered items, and NS for an empty essay.
- SCORE REPORTING: raw correct counts are the only fully defensible output. A 130-170 display must be labelled an unofficial estimate. Do not ship an equating table, a percentile lookup presented as ETS-equivalent, or an automated 0-6 essay score presented as exam-accurate — all five underlying inputs are unpublished.
- ESSAY UI: single 30-minute Analyze an Issue task with a restricted editor — insert, delete, cut/paste, undo only. Explicitly disable spellcheck and grammar check on the textarea (set spellcheck=false); leaving the browser default on makes the sim easier than the real test.
- QUESTION BANK TAGGING: tag against the eight domainSlug values supplied. Quantitative slugs map cleanly to ETS's four content areas; Verbal slugs map to ETS's three question types, which is how ETS itself structures Verbal content (it does not publish a separate Verbal content-domain tree). Exclude out-of-scope content at authoring time: no trigonometry, no calculus, no proof construction, no inferential statistics.
- FIGURE CONVENTIONS: encode a per-figure drawn-to-scale flag. Geometric figures are not necessarily to scale (but lines shown straight are straight, points are in the order shown, relative positions hold), while coordinate systems and statistical graphs ARE to scale and may legitimately be read by measurement. Explanations and distractor design depend on getting this right.
- CONTENT SOURCING GUARDRAIL: this research collected specifications only. Do not ingest ETS practice questions, passages, Issue pool prompts or answer keys into the bank — author original items tagged to the taxonomy above.
