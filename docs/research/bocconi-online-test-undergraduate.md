## Summary

The Online Bocconi Test is Bocconi University's own remote-proctored admission test for its Bachelor and integrated Master of Arts in Law programs. It exists in two variants — the "Online Bocconi Test" (valid for any program) and the "Online Bocconi Test - Law" (valid only for Law and Global Law) — each consisting of 50 multiple-choice questions to be completed in 75 minutes total, taken in Italian or English at the candidate's choice. It is NOT adaptive and NOT separately timed by section: questions from all content areas are interleaved and presented three per screen, and navigation is strictly forward-only with no return to a previous screen. Scoring is raw with negative marking (+1 correct, 0 omitted, −0.2 wrong, −0.33 for critical-thinking items with three options), and Bocconi publishes an official eligibility floor of 17 points below which an applicant is not considered. The Bocconi test is one of several accepted routes: SAT and ACT are accepted for Undergraduate School programs, and LSAT additionally for Law School programs, each with its own published minimum.

### Timing and breaks

One single continuous timed block of 75 minutes for all 50 questions: "The online Bocconi test lasts 75 minutes." Bocconi publishes NO per-section or per-screen time allocation, and there are no scheduled breaks — the rules of conduct forbid "leaving once the test has started" for the entire duration. At expiry the test auto-submits: "the test will close automatically when the available time has expired, the answers provided will be saved". Candidates with certified disabilities or specific learning disorders may be granted additional time following case-by-case assessment by a Commission; the amount of additional time is not published, and it is applied via a code entered at the moment of purchasing the test attempt. Candidates may take up to four attempts per test type per academic year, not on the same day nor on two consecutive days, and they select which attempt's score to submit with their application.


### Navigation and review

STRICTLY SEQUENTIAL AND FORWARD-ONLY. The official AY 2026-27 Instructions and Rules of Conduct, section 3.2 "SEQUENTIAL NAVIGATION DURING THE TEST", states: "The test offers 3 questions for each screen. With the "Next" button, you can view the next screen but going back to the previous one will no longer be possible." There is no flag-for-review or backward review facility across screens; within a single screen the three questions are simultaneously visible and presumably editable until Next is pressed, but Bocconi does not state this explicitly. The document warns that clicking through all questions without answering still consumes the attempt: "if you decide to go through all the test questions while not being able to go back, you will not be eligible for any refund". A "test navigation" box is present on all pages as a guide, but its capabilities are not described. At the end, "To end the test, click "Finish attempt". A summary page will appear with all questions, both answered and unanswered." followed by "Submit all and finish". Whether that summary page permits answering or changing previously unanswered/answered questions is NOT stated and must be treated as unverified — given the explicit forward-only rule, we assume it is view-only and disable any review-and-edit stage.


### Calculator

No calculator at any point. The official rules of conduct list among items NOT allowed: "having books, notebooks, notes, formularies, calculators or sources/resources available online". The only permitted materials on the desk are the identification document, 1 pencil/pen (no more), 2 blank A4 size sheets of paper (no notebooks and no more paper), and a drink in a glass or unlabelled water bottle, all of which must be shown to the camera on request. Headphones/earbuds are prohibited, as is any second internet-capable device beyond the mobile phone required for proctoring, and any second monitor even if switched off. The test runs inside the Safe Exam Browser lockdown browser, so no on-screen calculator is available either. Bocconi does not mention providing any digital scratchpad; scratch work is done on the two physical A4 sheets.


### Adaptive behaviour

NOT ADAPTIVE. Bocconi makes no claim of adaptive delivery, adaptive routing, IRT-based item selection, or module-level branching for this test. The official page describes a fixed linear form in which "The questions are distributed within the test in a mixed way both by difficulty level and by topic" — i.e. items of different areas and difficulties are interleaved across the 17 screens of 3 questions rather than grouped into blocks. Every candidate answers 50 questions in 75 minutes regardless of performance. Any product claim of proprietary adaptive scoring for the Bocconi test would be unsupported.


## Scoring

### Raw scoring

Raw scoring with negative marking, applied directly — there is no reported scaled score. Official statement: "Right answer: 1 point Missing answer: 0 points Wrong answer: — 0.2 points." An exception is published for critical thinking questions presented with three answer options, where "the penalty will be — 0.33 points". Omitted answers are therefore never penalised, which makes strategic omission rational and must be modelled. The maximum attainable total is 50 points (50 items x 1 point). The reported score is the total including penalties. Within 48 hours of finishing, the candidate can download a "test score report" from the platform showing the total score, the scores for the individual thematic areas (mathematics, comprehension of the text, critical and numerical reasoning), the number of correct / not answered / incorrect answers, and a "Reference number" identifying the attempt. Bocconi does NOT publish how many answer options each non-critical-thinking item carries, so the guessing-penalty geometry cannot be fully reconstructed.


### Reported scale

There is no raw-to-scaled conversion and no percentile or equated scale published for the Online Bocconi Test. The candidate receives the raw penalty-adjusted total (theoretical range roughly −10 to 50) plus per-area subscores. Two OFFICIAL eligibility floors exist and must be presented as floors, never as admission thresholds: (1) "Applicants who receive a total score (penalties included) lower than 17 will not be considered in the selection process."; (2) applicants listing the Bachelor in Mathematical and Computing Sciences for Artificial Intelligence among their choices "will need to obtain at least 11 points out of 24 in the "Mathematics" area". Admission itself is by competitive ranking, not by reaching a score: the test score contributes 55% and the third-last and second-last year high-school GPA contributes 45% of the overall selection score, and "The ranking is constructed according to an overall score determined by all the selection elements provided." Bocconi does NOT publish the formula that maps the raw 0–50 test score onto the 55% component, nor the GPA conversion onto the 45% component, nor any admission cut-off. Parallel official floors exist for the alternative tests: SAT total below 1040 not considered and section score below 520 in Evidence-Based Reading and Writing and Math not considered (600/800 Math for the AI bachelor; scores before 2022 not accepted; Essay not required); ACT composite below 19 not considered (25/36 Math for the AI bachelor; scores before 2021 not accepted).


### Not publicly documented — we do not reproduce these

- The formula converting the raw penalty-adjusted Bocconi test score (max 50) into the 55% component of the selection ranking is not published.
- The formula converting third-last and second-last year high-school GPA — across differing national grading systems — into the 45% component is not published.
- No equating, scaling or difficulty-adjustment procedure is published for the four permitted attempts, for different test forms, or between the Italian-language and English-language versions of the test. We cannot know whether two attempts are comparable.
- No normalisation table is published for comparing a Bocconi test score against an SAT, ACT or LSAT score when applicants using different tests are ranked together.
- No admission cut-off score is published for any programme, for any round. The only official numbers are the exclusion floors (17 total; 11/24 Mathematics for the AI bachelor; SAT 1040/520; ACT 19).
- The number of answer options per item is not published, except for the implicit statement that some critical thinking items have three options. The −0.2 penalty cannot be reverse-engineered into an option count with confidence.
- No IRT parameters, item difficulty statistics, or item-exposure rules are published — and none would apply, since the test is a fixed linear form and not adaptive.
- The additional-time multiplier granted to candidates with certified disabilities or specific learning disorders is not published; it is decided case by case by a Commission.

## What we could NOT verify

Each item below disables a dependent "exam-accurate" behaviour in the product. We would rather offer less than assert something false.

- Number of answer options per question. Only an indirect signal exists (some critical thinking items have three options, attracting a −0.33 penalty). The option count for Mathematics, Reading comprehension, Numerical reasoning and Verbal reasoning items is NOT published. Do not render a fixed 4- or 5-option layout as 'exam-accurate'.
- Whether the end-of-test summary page permits answering previously unanswered questions or editing answers, or is purely a view-only confirmation screen before 'Submit all and finish'.
- Whether answers can be changed among the three questions on the CURRENT screen before pressing Next. Only backward navigation across screens is explicitly forbidden.
- What the on-screen 'test navigation' box actually does — whether it merely shows progress or permits jumping forward to a later screen.
- Number of reading passages, their length, and how many of the 11 Reading comprehension questions attach to each passage. Passage grouping is implied but its shape is not published.
- The exact interleaving order of the content areas across the 17 screens. Bocconi says topics are 'mixed', so a simulation cannot reproduce the real ordering.
- Whether a countdown timer is displayed to the candidate during the test, and whether any per-screen time limit exists.
- Whether the four permitted attempts draw from equated forms of comparable difficulty, and whether the Italian and English versions are equated.
- The amount of additional time granted to candidates with certified disabilities or specific learning disorders (decided case by case; no published multiplier).
- The theoretical minimum score, i.e. whether the reported total is floored at zero or can be negative when penalties exceed correct answers.
- Whether the AY 2027-28 rules differ from the AY 2026-27 rules on navigation, conduct or attempt limits — the 2027-28 Instructions and Rules of Conduct PDF was not retrievable (HTTP 403 on the guessed URL, absent from search).
- Whether a Spring session formally exists for the AY 2027-28 international cycle. The admissions page lists only Early and Winter for 2027-28, but the test page allows Italian applicants to book until 20 April 2027, and the results page shows a Spring session in its (2025-26) structure.

## Implementation notes

- TIMING: single module, hard 75:00 countdown covering all 50 items. No per-section timers, no breaks, no pause. On expiry, auto-submit and persist all answers given so far — this mirrors the real platform ('the test will close automatically when the available time has expired'). Do not implement a grace period.
- NAVIGATION: implement a 'forward_only_paged' mode with pageSize = 3. Within a page, all three items are answerable and editable. Pressing Next commits the page irreversibly. Disable Previous, disable flag-for-review, disable a question-grid jump control. This is the single most distinctive behaviour of this exam and the main thing a generic engine gets wrong.
- REVIEW STAGE: render a final view-only summary listing every item as answered or unanswered, then a 'Submit all and finish' confirm. Do NOT let the learner answer from that summary — Bocconi does not say it is possible and the forward-only rule implies it is not. Mark this behaviour as 'assumed strict' in the config so it can be relaxed if the 2027-28 PDF says otherwise.
- SCORING: per-item raw scoring in decimals. correct = +1.0; omitted = 0.0; incorrect = −0.2, EXCEPT three-option critical thinking items where incorrect = −0.33. Store the penalty on the item, not the exam. Report the penalty-inclusive total out of 50 plus per-area subscores matching the real score report (mathematics, comprehension of the text, critical reasoning, numerical reasoning). Add unit tests asserting a blank test scores 0 and a fully correct test scores exactly 50.
- SCORING GUARDRAIL: the 17-point floor is an exclusion threshold, not a pass mark and not an admission predictor. Render it with its citation and the exact phrasing 'will not be considered in the selection process'. Never compute or display an admission probability, and never surface a 'target score'. Build no historical-average widget for Bocconi — no official distribution exists.
- ANSWER OPTIONS: the number of choices per item is not published. Author original items with a documented in-house convention, label the simulation's option count as a product choice rather than an exam-accurate detail, and keep optionCount a per-item field so it can be corrected later without a data migration.
- ADAPTIVITY: adaptive.enabled = false. Item selection for the simulation must be a fixed blueprint (24/11/6/9 or 5/11/6/18/10) with items interleaved by topic and difficulty to match 'distributed ... in a mixed way'. Adaptive logic is permitted only in non-simulation practice drills, and must be labelled a study feature, not exam behaviour.
- CALCULATOR: calculator = 'none' for every section of both variants. Suppress the calculator affordance entirely rather than disabling a visible button.
- TAXONOMY: tag the question bank against the nine domainSlug values above. The Mathematics domain is unusually deep (33 published subtopics) and carries 24 of 50 items in the standard variant, so weight content production accordingly — nearly half the standard test is maths.
- TWO BLUEPRINTS, ONE ENGINE CONFIG: share timing, navigation, scoring and calculator settings between the standard and Law variants; vary only the content blueprint and the eligible-programme list. Gate the Law variant behind a 'Law and Global Law' programme selection.
- PROGRAMME RULES: implement subscore-threshold rules keyed to (programme, route, section) so the Mathematical and Computing Sciences for Artificial Intelligence requirement (11/24 Bocconi Math, 600/800 SAT Math, 25/36 ACT Math) is expressible without special-casing.
- ROUTE COMPARISON: build a Bocconi admissions hub presenting the Bocconi test, SAT, ACT and LSAT as parallel routes with their official floors side by side, each citing its own official URL. Separate 'admission test' content from 'English certificate for enrollment' content — conflating them is a common third-party error.
- CYCLE LABELLING: store cycle + sourceUrl + verifiedOn per fact. Surface 'Verified 18 September 2026 against Bocconi's AY 2027-28 pages; delivery and conduct rules from the AY 2026-27 official rules document' on every Bocconi page, and schedule re-verification when the AY 2027-28 Instructions and Rules of Conduct PDF is published.
- DISABLE UNTIL VERIFIED: passage grouping shape, on-screen timer presence, and whether the summary page is editable are unverified. Pick a defensible default for each, flag it in the config as assumed rather than verified, and scope the 'exam-accurate simulation' badge to the four things that ARE verified: 50 items, 75 minutes, forward-only 3-per-screen paging, and the penalty scheme.
- ACCESSIBILITY: extra time is granted case by case by Bocconi with no published multiplier, so offer accommodation settings as a generic study aid (e.g. 1.25x/1.5x) explicitly labelled as not replicating any official Bocconi allowance.
- PROCTORING: do not attempt to replicate Safe Exam Browser, webcam or ProctorExam behaviour — the brief already forbids invasive proctoring in the initial release. Instead, describe the real proctoring setup on the guide page so learners are not surprised on test day (second device, room 360-degree scan, ears visible, single closed door, no headphones).
